Games I Play

What you'll find on stream and why I play it

My stream rotates between a few core games and occasional new releases. I tend to stick with games long enough to actually get good at them — I'm not a variety streamer in the "play every new game once" sense. The games below are the ones I keep coming back to, each for different reasons. Some are competitive, some are relaxed, and some are just genuinely great games that hold up no matter how many hours you put in.

Star Wars Battlefront II

PC Multiplayer Main Game

Star Wars Battlefront II is my most-streamed game and one I genuinely love playing. Despite its controversial launch in 2017, the game has evolved into a polished and genuinely great multiplayer experience. The combination of large-scale infantry battles, vehicle combat, and hero gameplay creates variety within a single session that keeps things interesting for both me and viewers.

I primarily play Heroes vs. Villains, Galactic Assault, and Supremacy. Heroes vs. Villains is the most intense — a 4v4 format where each team has one hero or villain and the rest play as elite troopers. The limited number of players means every decision matters, and the hero gameplay is dynamic enough that no two matches feel the same.

My main heroes are Luke Skywalker on the light side and Boba Fett on the dark side. Luke rewards aggressive, close-range play — his Rush, Repulse, and Saber Throw abilities give him tools for almost every situation. Boba Fett is a completely different style: aerial positioning, ranged damage, and controlling space from above rather than diving into the middle of a fight. I enjoy playing both because they require fundamentally different approaches.

In the larger modes, I run the Officer class as my primary trooper. Officer is a team support role — you're providing Battle Command buffs, deploying turrets at key positions, and keeping squad members alive through passive healing. It's not always the highest-kill playstyle but it's consistently high-impact, and there's something satisfying about winning an objective fight because your team was better supported.

If you want more detail on my loadouts and strategy, the Battlefront Stats page covers everything. I stream SWBF2 regularly on Twitch — come watch and ask questions in chat.

Nintendo Switch

Console Variety

The Nintendo Switch is a constant in my setup. It's the most versatile gaming device I own — at home it's a full console, on the road it's a handheld, and the library covers almost every genre and mood. For streaming, the Switch is particularly good because the games tend to be immediately accessible to viewers regardless of their own gaming background.

Mario Kart 8 Deluxe is one of my go-to titles for interactive streams. The races are short, the chaos is unpredictable, and it's easy to involve viewers by letting chat vote on characters, courses, or items. Even people who don't play games themselves tend to have opinions about Mario Kart, which makes it one of the best games for generating chat activity.

Super Smash Bros. Ultimate is another regular. The roster size alone generates conversation — there's almost always someone in chat who mains whoever you're playing, which leads to genuine back-and-forth about character strengths, matchups, and strategy. I enjoy the competitive side of Smash and I'm always working on improving, which gives the streams a sense of progression over time.

Beyond those, I pick up new titles when they interest me and fit the kind of content I want to make. The Switch library is deep enough that there's always something new to explore, and first-time playthroughs of games I haven't tried before tend to make for some of the most entertaining stream content.

What to Expect on Stream

Regardless of what game I'm playing, the stream atmosphere stays consistent: relaxed, conversational, and respectful. I genuinely enjoy interacting with chat and I don't ignore it to focus purely on gameplay — finding the balance between playing well and engaging with viewers is something I've worked on a lot.

I don't rage, I don't force reactions, and I don't play a character. What you see on stream is how I actually am while playing games. If something is funny, it's genuinely funny. If a match goes badly, I'll reflect on what went wrong rather than blaming lag or teammates. That approach keeps the stream atmosphere positive and is the kind of content I actually want to watch myself.

Highlights and clips from streams go up on YouTube and TikTok if you missed a session. Follow on Twitch to get notified when I go live.
